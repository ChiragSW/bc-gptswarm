\section{Limitations}

The reproduction cannot access the original 2024 model snapshots. A reduced MMLU subset has limited power, while broader GPTSwarm claims about tool use and node optimization are not reproduced. The local model is deterministic under the configured seed, but system scheduling and CPU load still affect wall time.

The contribution is deliberately narrow because closely related work already studies efficient routing, pruning, and model substitution. An expected-token constraint does not impose a per-query hard cap and may be satisfied on average while individual executions exceed $B$. Token count also imperfectly represents latency, energy, or monetary cost. Discrete graphs, binary rewards, and stochastic model outputs destabilize primal--dual learning. We mitigate this with a measured feasibility floor, normalized violation, validity constraints, and complete trajectory logging, but the low and middle budgets still do not converge under twelve iterations.

The Phase 4 behavioral reproduction uses a 0.5B local model, 24 evaluation questions, 12 optimizer iterations, and a 1T1A graph. It is consequently underpowered for claims about the original model or full GPTSwarm setting. A small model may also produce too little positive reward for stable raw-utility policy gradients. Conditions ran in a fixed order, so local-model warm-up confounds cross-method latency. These limitations make the null optimized-versus-random result evidence about this implementation setting, not evidence against GPTSwarm under its published conditions.

The Phase 5 constrained smoke run is still weaker evidence: its provider is deterministic and synthetic, its initial dual value and larger mock batch were chosen to make implementation faults observable, and only five held-out examples are sampled. Mean compliance does not imply per-query compliance; two of five smoke executions exceed the target. Its numbers must not be compared with Phase 4 or used to select Phase 6 settings after seeing real-model outcomes.

Phases 6--7 use only three seeds and 24 held-out questions per condition while examining multiple budgets and ablations. Resulting confidence intervals are broad. With three independent pairs, an exact two-sided sign-flip test cannot attain $p<0.25$, and Cohen's $d_z$ is unstable. The secondary McNemar analysis conditions on the three fitted policies; clustering its interval by question does not replace independent optimizer replications. No confirmatory ablation family or equivalence margin was preregistered, so multiplicity-adjusted ablation tests and equivalence claims are inappropriate. The same local model often answers incorrectly regardless of graph: both paired methods fail on 52 of 72 seed--question cases. One retained optimization wall-time outlier further limits timing conclusions. These results establish behavior only for the declared reduced setting.
