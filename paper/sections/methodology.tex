Both signals update the edge policy, but only measured cost updates the dual variable. Node routines, prompts, the cycle-rejecting sampler, and the decision node remain baseline components.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Objective}

The reproduction retains GPTSwarm's accuracy-only edge objective as a baseline. BC-GPTSwarm preserves its edge distribution $D_{\theta}$ but constrains measured resource use. For input $x$, sampled feasible graph $G\sim D_{\theta}$, task utility $U(G,x)$, total-token cost $C(G,x)$, and target $B>0$, the proposed problem is
\begin{align}
    \max_{\theta}\quad & \mathrm{E}_{x,G}[U(G,x)] \\
    \text{subject to}\quad & \mathrm{E}_{x,G}[C(G,x)] \leq B.
\end{align}
Using the normalized constraint $g=\mathrm{E}[C/B-1]\leq0$, its Lagrangian is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{L}(\theta,\lambda)
 &=\mathrm{E}_{x,G}\!\left[U(G,x)\right. \notag\\
 &\left.\quad-\lambda\left(\frac{C(G,x)}{B}-1\right)\right],\\
 \lambda&\geq0.
\end{align}
Normalization makes the dual scale comparable across budgets; it does not remove the need to predeclare optimizer settings.

The fixed-penalty control instead uses
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\theta}\;\mathrm{E}_{x,G}\!\left[
 U(G,x)-\alpha\frac{C(G,x)}{B_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right],
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is fixed before evaluation. Unlike BC-GPTSwarm, this scalarized objective has no mechanism that targets a requested constraint value.

\subsection{Primal--Dual Update}

For $M$ sampled executions, the planned edge-policy estimate is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}}=
 \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}
 \left[A_i-\lambda\left(\frac{C_i}{B}-1\right)\right]
 \nabla_{\theta}\log p_{\theta}(G_i),
\end{equation}
where $A_i$ is a centered task-utility advantage. The dual variable follows projected dual descent, equivalently increasing its penalty when the observed constraint is violated:
\begin{equation}
 \lambda\leftarrow\left[\lambda+\eta_{\lambda}
 \left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{C_i}{B}-1\right)\right]_{+}.
\end{equation}
The primary cost $C$ is the sum of reported input and output tokens across model calls. Dollar cost and latency are recorded but excluded from the initial constraint to keep the intervention identifiable.

\subsection{Planned Optimization Procedure}

For each target budget and seed, the proposed procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item calibrate a valid cost floor and freeze $B$, model settings, prompts, and candidate edges;
    \item sample $M$ feasible DAGs from $D_{\theta}$ for the current optimization batch;
    \item execute every sampled graph, recording task utility and per-call token metadata;
    \item compute the centered utility advantage and normalized budget violation;
    \item update edge logits by the primal policy-gradient estimate and update $\lambda$ by projected dual descent; and
    \item after the fixed optimization allowance, evaluate the frozen edge policy on held-out examples.
\end{enumerate}
The final implementation will expose the primal and dual learning rates, batch size, budget, feasibility rule, and random seed in configuration. A deterministic mock model will be used to check gradient signs before paid or nondeterministic inference.

\subsection{Feasibility and Fairness}

A disconnected or zero-call graph is not a valid budget solution. A smoke test will estimate the minimum cost of a valid direct-answer path; lower budgets will be declared infeasible. Every sampled graph must retain an input-to-decision path. This feasibility rule, the candidate edges, and the optimization-call allowance will be identical for accuracy-only, fixed-penalty, and constrained methods. No proposed method has yet been implemented, and all quantitative claims remain pending experiment.
