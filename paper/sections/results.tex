\section{Results}

\subsection{Implementation Validation}

Eleven offline tests pass: six baseline tests, four constrained-method tests, and one controlled-objective test. They cover deterministic edge order, stochastic DAG validity, answer parsing, dataset loading, finite gradients, feasible conditional sampling, threshold repair, dual projection, cost-sensitive policy behavior, and fixed-dual controls. Deterministic-mock and local-Ollama smoke tiers complete without structural failures. The Phase 5 mock smoke averages 117.6 tokens against its 130-token expected budget, but remains software validation rather than research evidence.

\subsection{Baseline Reproduction}

\input{tables/baseline_results}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline-results} records the Phase 4 publication-style baseline. Accuracy-only optimized and random graphs both average 0.250 accuracy. Full connectivity reaches 0.264 and direct answer 0.167. Optimized graphs use 6,027 tokens over 24 questions, 17.2\% fewer than full graphs, but 35.0\% more than random graphs. Random sampling averages 4.7 disconnected failures, making some apparent efficiency a failure artifact.

\subsection{Controlled Main Comparison}

\input{tables/main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports all 18 Phase 6 runs. The original accuracy-only baseline has the highest main-study mean accuracy, 0.250. Middle-budget BC-GPTSwarm reaches 0.236, a difference of $-0.014$, while using 236.7 versus 251.1 tokens per question, a 5.8\% reduction. Its normalized budget violation is nevertheless $+0.040$. This is a small descriptive tradeoff, not an accuracy improvement.

The low target is raised by the predeclared feasibility floor from 151.6 to 180.4 tokens, yet BC-GPTSwarm still exceeds it by 28.8\% on average. The high-budget condition averages 238.9 tokens against 303.3 and is the only constrained setting with clear aggregate slack. Per-query compliance increases from 0.38 to 0.50 to 0.75 as the target increases. Feasible accuracy-only and fixed-penalty controls average 0.222 and 0.208 accuracy. All system and graph failure counts are zero.

\subsection{Budget and Mechanism Ablations}

\input{tables/ablation_results}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} reports Phase 7 ablations. Raw rather than centered utility reaches 0.250 accuracy and 232.4 tokens, versus 0.236 and 236.7 for centered utility, but its accuracy standard deviation is 0.150. Dual rates 0.05, 0.20, and 0.50 produce mean accuracy 0.222, 0.236, and 0.222 and normalized violation $+0.027$, $+0.040$, and $+0.009$. No rate has a stable accuracy advantage.

The 2T2A graph reaches 0.278 accuracy versus 0.236 for 1T1A, but uses 419.5 rather than 236.7 tokens and violates the middle target by 84.4\%. More agents therefore buy a small mean accuracy increase without resource control.

\subsection{Optimization Stability}

Final mean dual values for budget ratios 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00 are 1.129, 0.427, and 0.005. This ordering is mechanically correct: tighter budgets create larger penalties. However, final training-batch cost ratios remain 1.506, 1.321, and 0.950. The fixed 12-iteration allowance is adequate only for the high target and does not establish tight-budget convergence.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{../results/figures/optimization_stability.pdf}
    \caption{Mean normalized training-batch cost and dual trajectory over three seeds. The dashed line is the expected-budget boundary.}
    \label{fig:optimization-stability}
\end{figure*}

Across all 30 main and ablation runs, active-agent count correlates 0.424 with accuracy, active-edge count 0.224, tokens 0.420, and latency 0.287. These run-level Pearson correlations are descriptive and strongly influenced by the 2T2A condition. The descriptive mean-point Pareto set contains the high-dual, raw-utility, and 2T2A variants; their uncertainty prevents ranking them as statistically distinct systems.

\subsection{Paired Outcomes and Failures}

Pairing original accuracy-only and middle-budget BC-GPTSwarm by seed and question gives 72 comparisons: both are correct in 15, both are wrong in 52, the baseline alone is correct in three, and BC-GPTSwarm alone is correct in two. The five disagreements span high-school computer science, global facts, high-school microeconomics, and electrical engineering. In the electrical-engineering baseline-only win, BC-GPTSwarm uses 306 tokens versus 148 for the baseline, showing that extra calls can hurt both cost and correctness.

There are no provider errors, unparseable final answers, or disconnected graphs in Phases 6--7. The dominant failure mode is ordinary task error from the small local model. Feasibility conditioning removes structural failures but does not fix weak reasoning.
