\section{Conclusion}

We reproduce GPTSwarm's adversarial MMLU edge optimizer and implement BC-GPTSwarm, a feasible primal--dual extension for an explicit expected token budget. Across the controlled reduced study, original accuracy-only optimization averages 0.250 accuracy. Middle-budget BC-GPTSwarm averages 0.236 while using 5.8\% fewer tokens, but it remains 4.0\% over budget. The low target is not achieved, and only the high target has clear mean slack. Ablations show no stable advantage from the default dual rate or utility centering; a larger 2T2A graph raises both accuracy and cost and is strongly infeasible.

The implementation contribution is supported: conditional graph sampling is valid, structural failures are eliminated, resource telemetry is complete, and the learned dual increases under budget pressure. The performance hypothesis is not supported in this CPU-feasible setting. Three seeds, 24 questions, a small local model, and twelve optimizer iterations leave broad uncertainty and incomplete convergence. Future work should treat a longer predeclared optimization allowance and a stronger graph-sensitive task as new experiments, not as post-hoc repairs to these results.
